Renovation tax in Spain: what you pay the council

ICIO, the processing fee and the refundable waste deposit — what they are, how much, and when you don’t pay.

In short: when you file a notification or application for a renovation, three things are paid: ICIO tax (4% of the works cost in Barcelona), a processing fee, and a construction waste deposit that is refunded afterwards. A skip on the street adds a fee for the space.

1. ICIO tax

ICIO (Impost sobre construccions, instal·lacions i obres) is a municipal tax on building work.

  • How much: a percentage of the works cost. By law, councils can’t set more than 4%. Barcelona charges 4%.
  • On what: the cost of the work and materials (presupuesto de ejecución material), excluding taxes and the contractor’s profit.
  • When it doesn’t apply: in Barcelona, interior work that doesn’t change layout, structure or façade is usually not subject to ICIO. Replacing tiles, floors, sanitaryware in the same places — no ICIO.
  • Example: layout change, works of €20,000 — ICIO about €800.

Other towns (L’Hospitalet, Badalona, Tarragona, Salou) set their own rate, but never above 4%.

2. Processing fee

The taxa per serveis urbanístics pays for the council checking your documents. It’s separate from ICIO. In Barcelona, under the 2025 ordinance: €43 or €51 for a comunicat (with a technical project), from €385 for a works licence. Other towns have their own amounts.

3. Construction waste deposit

In Catalonia, a deposit for proper waste disposal (fiança de residus) is paid with the permit. It’s not a tax — you get it back.

  1. You pay the deposit when filing. The amount depends on the volume of waste; some towns set a minimum (for example, around €150).
  2. The rubble goes to an authorised recycling site (gestor autoritzat).
  3. The site issues a certificate of how much waste it received.
  4. The certificate goes to the council after the works — and the deposit is refunded.

No certificate, no refund. Make sure your contractor uses an authorised site and gives you the paper.

4. A skip on the street

A skip or large bags on the pavement need a permit to occupy public space, with its own fee. Usually the skip company arranges it.
