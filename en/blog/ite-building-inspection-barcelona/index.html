ITE: the old-building inspection and your renovation

Building over 45 years old? What the ITE is, why to check it before buying and how it affects your renovation.

In short: in Catalonia every residential building over 45 years old must pass a technical inspection — the ITE (Inspecció Tècnica de l’Edifici). If you’re buying or renovating in an old building, find out its result: it drives future costs for all owners.

What the ITE is

An architect or technical architect inspects the common parts: façade, roof, structure, risers, balconies. They write a report (IITE), and the Generalitat issues a certificate of fitness (certificat d’aptitud).

  • When: before the building turns 45. Age is taken from the cadastre.
  • Certificate for 10 years — if there are no serious defects.
  • Provisional, for 6 years — if important defects were found. They must be fixed.
  • No ITE — under the housing law this is a serious infringement and can be fined.

Barcelona has many old buildings: Eixample, Gràcia, Sants, Poblenou. Almost all should already have passed the ITE.

Why it matters for your renovation

  1. Common works are shared. If the ITE found problems with the façade, roof or risers, the repair is split between owners — possibly a sizeable sum on top of your own renovation.
  2. Risers. If the building will soon replace shared pipes, it makes sense to time your bathroom and kitchen with that work.
  3. Balconies and façade. You can’t change them yourself: they’re common elements. First, the owners’ association decides.

If you’re buying

If the building must have an ITE, the seller must give the buyer the certificate and a copy of the report. Ask for them before signing and check:

  • the result: no defects or with defects;
  • which works are recommended and by when;
  • whether the association has agreed to do them and how much you’ll pay.
