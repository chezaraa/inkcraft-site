Renovating an apartment for rental: what pays off

What to invest in, where to save, and which materials survive tenants.

In short: a rental renovation isn’t “the cheap version” but “one that pays back and lasts”. Spend on what shows in photos and what tenants break; save on what they won’t notice.

Where to invest

  • Kitchen and bathroom. First thing people look at in the listing and at the viewing.
  • Floors. Porcelain tiles or tough laminate/vinyl — they survive moves and suitcases.
  • Lighting. Good lighting makes listing photos much better.
  • Storage. Floor-to-ceiling wardrobes — the flat feels bigger and tidier.
  • Air conditioning. In a Barcelona summer it matters to tenants.

Where to save

  • Designer sanitaryware and expensive taps — choose reliable mid-range.
  • Complex decorative details that are easy to damage.
  • Light fabrics and matt paints that show marks — use washable finishes.

Materials that survive tenants

  • Porcelain instead of wood on the floor.
  • Washable wall paint.
  • Quartz or ceramic worktop instead of wood.
  • Near the sea — stainless hardware and moisture-resistant fronts.
