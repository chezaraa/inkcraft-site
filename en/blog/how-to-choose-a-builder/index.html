How to choose a renovation company in Barcelona

A checklist before you sign and the warning signs to watch for.

In short: look at real projects and reviews, ask for an itemised quote and a written contract with price and timeline, pay in stages and agree on reports. The cheapest quote is almost never the cheapest at the end.

Checklist before you sign

  1. Real projects. Ask for photos and video of their own work, not pictures from the internet. Before/after of the same apartment is ideal.
  2. Google reviews. Real reviews with names and details. Five reviews on one day with no detail is a bad sign.
  3. Itemised quote. Not “apartment renovation — €40,000” but line by line: demolition, electrics, plumbing, tiles, materials.
  4. Written contract. Scope, price, timeline, payment schedule, guarantee.
  5. Staged payments. A deposit to start, then for completed stages. Never 100% up front.
  6. One responsible person. Who answers for the result and the deadline? If it’s “different trades, you deal with them”, you’ll be the coordinator.
  7. Reports. How often will you get photos and video? Especially if you’re not in Barcelona.
  8. Permits. The contractor should tell you which permit your work needs. “Nobody checks” is a red flag. Permits →
  9. Guarantee. How long, and on what exactly.

Red flags

  • A price far below everyone else’s with no clear reason.
  • A quote “without seeing it” without seeing the apartment.
  • A large deposit “for materials” with no list of materials.
  • No project they can show you.
  • A timeline of “we’ll see”.

Why a cheap quote gets expensive

It leaves out what “comes up later”: new pipes, wall levelling, rubbish removal, carrying materials without a lift. Mid-way it turns into extras, and you can’t walk away — the apartment is stripped. An honest quote reflects the apartment’s condition from the start.
