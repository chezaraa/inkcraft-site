Renovation and neighbours: what the owners’ association allows

What you can do without your neighbours’ consent, what needs it, and how to renovate without conflict.

In short: inside your own apartment you can renovate without the neighbours’ permission, as long as you don’t touch the structure, façade or common parts. But you are legally required to inform the association’s president or administrator before you start.

What the law says

In Spain, life in a building is governed by the Horizontal Property Law (Ley de Propiedad Horizontal). Under article 7, an owner may alter their apartment if:

  • the building’s safety and structure are not affected;
  • its exterior (façade, balconies, windows) does not change;
  • other owners’ rights are not harmed.

And one more condition: you must notify the works in advance to the association’s representative — usually the president or administrator (administrador de fincas).

Catalonia also has its own Civil Code with similar rules. Check the procedure with your building administrator.

Fine without the neighbours’ consent

  • Painting, floors, doors.
  • A new kitchen and bathroom.
  • New electrics and pipes inside the apartment.
  • Moving non-load-bearing partitions (needs a council permit, not the neighbours).

Needs the association’s consent

  • Load-bearing walls and floors.
  • Façade, balconies, glazing a terrace, windows that look different.
  • An outdoor AC unit on the façade — depends on the building’s rules.
  • Shared risers, roof, entrance, lift.

How to keep the peace

  1. Inform the administrator — in writing, with a description and dates.
  2. Put up a notice in the entrance: what, when, and a contact number.
  3. Noisy work on weekdays, in the daytime. In Catalonia construction machinery runs from 8:00 to 20:00. Weekends — quiet work only.
  4. Protect the lift and stairs — cardboard, film. Rubble in bags, never left in the hallway.
  5. Water shut-offs — warn the neighbours in advance.
