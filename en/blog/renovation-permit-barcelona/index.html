Renovation permits in Barcelona: what you need and how long it takes

Finishes only, moving partitions or touching load-bearing walls — each case has its own permit in Barcelona. Explained simply.

In short: renovating an apartment in Barcelona almost always requires notifying the Ajuntament. Finishes only: a simple notification (assabentat). Moving walls: comunicat immediat — you can start right after filing and paying. Touching load-bearing structure or a protected building: comunicat diferit (up to 1 month) or a full licence (llicència d’obres majors).

Four types of permit

PermitWhenWhen you can start
Assabentat
minor works, type III		No change to layout, structure or façade: tiles, floors, painting, replacing kitchen and bathroom in the same places.Right after filing.
Comunicat immediat
minor works, type II		Layout changes inside the apartment: removing and moving partitions, opening the kitchen to the living room.Right after filing and paying the tax.
Comunicat diferit
minor works, type I		Work that touches load-bearing elements locally, and some work in protected buildings.The council reviews for up to 1 month.
Llicència d’obres majorsMajor structural or façade work, rebuilding.After the licence is granted, typically about 2 months.

The exact type for your apartment depends on the scope of work and whether the building is listed; the council website has a preliminary check.

What it costs

  • ICIO (local construction tax) — 4% of the works cost in Barcelona. Interior work that doesn’t change layout, structure or façade is usually exempt.
  • Processing fee (taxa per serveis urbanístics) — under the 2025 ordinance, €43 or €51 for a comunicat, from €385 for a licence.
  • Technical project — layout and structural changes need a project by a qualified technician.

Key deadlines

  • Under a comunicat, the work must be finished within 6 months of the start date stated in the application.
  • Comunicat diferit: up to 1 month of review, no work before that.
  • Major works licence: allow about 2 months, more including the project.

Also consider

  • The owners’ association. Tell the building administrator: many buildings have rules on working hours, the lift and rubbish. Renovation and neighbours →
  • A skip on the street needs a separate permit to occupy public space.
  • No permit = risk. The council can stop the work and fine you, and an illegal layout change surfaces when you sell.
