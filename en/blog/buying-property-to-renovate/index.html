Buying an apartment to renovate in Spain: what to check first

A buyer’s checklist: what to look at in the apartment and the building so the renovation doesn’t become a nasty surprise.

In short: a property to renovate in Spain is usually cheaper, but before buying you need to know what the renovation will cost. Check pipes, wiring, windows, the building’s condition and any debts to the owners’ association — and bring a contractor to the viewing.

In the apartment

  1. Pipes. Old metal pipes are almost always replaced. Look under the sink and in the bathroom: rust, leaks, ceiling stains.
  2. Electrics. An old fuse box and unearthed sockets mean full rewiring.
  3. Damp. Stains, mould, a musty smell — especially near the sea and on ground floors.
  4. Windows. Old aluminium frames are noisy, cold in winter and hot in summer. Replacing them adds noticeably to the budget. Window replacement →
  5. Layout. Want to remove walls? Find out which are load-bearing — it decides the permit. More →
  6. Ceilings and floors. Old buildings have timber beams and vaults. Beautiful, but check their condition.

In the building

  • ITE inspection. For older buildings — has it been done and what did it find? Façade or roof repairs are shared between owners. About ITE →
  • Owners’ association. Any debts on the apartment, and any expensive planned works (lift, façade, risers)?
  • Lift. Without one, the renovation costs more: carrying materials up and rubble down.
  • Listed building. In some areas (e.g. Eixample) buildings are protected — permits take longer.

Documents to ask for

  • Nota simple — the Land Registry extract: who owns it, any mortgage or charges.
  • Owners’ association debt certificate (certificado de deudas) and any agreed but unpaid levies for building works (derramas).
  • Cédula de habitabilitat — in Catalonia, usually required to sell or rent out a home.
  • Energy performance certificate (certificado de eficiencia energética) — the seller must provide it.
  • Aluminosis. Some Barcelona buildings from the 1950s–70s were built with high-alumina cement concrete, which can deteriorate. If the building is from that period, ask whether it has been checked.

Getting the budget right

  • Bring a contractor before you sign. They see what a buyer doesn’t and give a price guide.
  • Allow 10–15% contingency for what turns up after demolition.
  • Count the time. During the works you pay rent elsewhere or lose rental income. Timelines →
